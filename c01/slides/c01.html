Two kinds of language

What is a natural language? Bangla, English, Arabic, French: the languages people speak. In a natural language, the language comes first and the grammar later: people speak first; grammar describes the language afterwards. Then what is an unnatural language? The opposite of natural: formal. In a formal language the order is reversed: grammar first, then the language. The rules are written first, and the language follows them.

Four formal languages, in the order they appear. Algebra: (a + b) squared = a squared + 2ab + b squared. Logic: for every x, if x is a student, then there is a y such that x is enrolled in y. A legal contract: defined terms, numbered clauses, no pronouns. Code: for i in range(10): print(i). Each symbol has one meaning.

Why natural language is hard

কাল

আমি কাল যাবtomorrow
EnglishI saw her duck.
  • Ambiguityone word, many meanings
  • World knowledge“The box was in the pen.”Bar-Hillel, 1960
  • Variationspelling, dialect, new words
  • No complete grammarnobody has written all the rules

Why natural language is hard. Ambiguity: one word, many meanings, depending on context. The Bangla word কাল means yesterday in আমি কাল গিয়েছিলাম and tomorrow in আমি কাল যাব. Only the verb tells which. English: “I saw her duck.” Her duck, the bird; or she ducked. World knowledge: “The box was in the pen.” (Bar-Hillel, 1960). To read pen correctly, as a pen for animals or children and not a writing pen, the reader must know how big a box is. Variation: spelling, dialect, new words. No complete grammar: nobody has written all the rules.

What value goes in each position?

A text of any length becomes one vector x in R^|V|: one position per vocabulary word. The toy vocabulary has seven words in a fixed order: 1 a, 2 beat, 3 dhaka, 4 film, 5 in, 6 india, 7 new.

First text, dhaka beat india: a text of three words. Its vector has seven positions, one for each word of the vocabulary; the positions start empty. dhaka goes to position 3, beat to position 2, india to position 6. The vocabulary fixes the position, not the order of the words in the text.

Second text, a new film in dhaka: five words. The same seven positions, in the same order: a 1, new 7, film 4, in 5, dhaka 3. 5 of 7 positions used; the length is always 7.

Our corpus: 111,441 word types in one list, Bangla and English, so x is in R^111,441. |V| is the size of the vocabulary, the number of word types. Each position holds one number about its word. What value goes in each position? The next slide answers: the count.

Two problems of raw counts.

Length. The same text written twice has twice the counts: dhaka beat india gives beat 1, dhaka 1, india 1; the same text twice gives 2, 2, 2. Solution: scale each row to length 1. The length of (1, 1, 1) is the square root of 3, about 1.73; the length of (2, 2, 2) is the square root of 12, about 3.46, twice as long. Divided by its length, each row becomes (0.58, 0.58, 0.58), because 1 / 1.73 = 0.58: the same vector.

Common words. dhaka and in are in all three toy texts. In the bag of words, row 1 has dhaka = 2: the largest count, but dhaka is not a topic word. “the” or এবং counts as much as ক্রিকেট. Solution: weight each word by how rare it is. That weight is idf, on the next slide.

Inverse document frequency: idf(w) = log(N / df(w)). The formula computes in place, one word per step: w becomes the word, N becomes 3, the texts that contain the word light up and df becomes their number, then the log and the result; the point lands on the plot. At the end the formula returns to its general form. N: number of texts. df(w): number of texts that contain w.

The three toy texts, N = 3: dhaka beat india in dhaka; india beat dhaka in dhaka; a new film in dhaka.

a, film, new are in one text: df = 1, idf = log 3 = 1.10. beat, india are in two texts: df = 2, idf = log 1.5 = 0.41. dhaka, in are in all three texts: df = 3, idf = log 1 = 0.00. The values use the natural logarithm.

From counts to TF-IDF: start from the counts, then two steps. TF is the term frequency: the count of a word in a text. Start from the counts: the bag of words of the three toy texts (rows 1, 2, 3).

Step 1: count × idf. Multiply each count by the idf of its word (the row above the words). dhaka and in have idf 0.00, so they become 0 in every row, even dhaka = 2. Rows 1 and 2: beat 1 × 0.41 = 0.41 and india 1 × 0.41 = 0.41; the other entries are 0. Row 3: a, film and new, 1 × 1.10 = 1.10; the other entries are 0. Rarer words retain weight.

Step 2: scale each row to length one. Divide every entry by the length of its row. Row 1: length = √(0.41² + 0.41²) = 0.57. Divide each entry by 0.57: 0.41 / 0.57 = 0.71. Row 2: the same. Row 3: length = √(1.10² + 1.10² + 1.10²) = 1.90. Divide each entry by 1.90: 1.10 / 1.90 = 0.58. (With the rounded 1.10 a student gets 1.10 × √3 = 1.91; the slide uses log 3 = 1.099, so the length is 1.903 ≈ 1.90, and 1.099 / 1.903 = 0.58.) If a student gets 0.58 and 0.72 for row 1: the slide uses the unrounded idf, log 1.5 = 0.405, so the length is 0.573 and 0.405 / 0.573 = 0.71.

Colours: grey is 0; in each step, the darker the blue, the larger the value. The bars on the right show each row’s length to scale: after the division every row has length 1.

Cosine similarity: the angle between two texts.

Every row has length one. So the dot product of two rows is the cosine of the angle between them: cos θ = x · y / (‖x‖ ‖y‖) = x · y.

In the picture both arrows have length 1, so both end on the grey arc. The blue segment is cos θ: how far y reaches along x.

1 → same words, same proportions: the two arrows point the same way, the angle is 0. 0 → no weighted word shared: the arrows are at right angles.

The toy texts after TF-IDF: row 1 = row 2 = (0, 0.71, 0, 0, 0, 0.71, 0); row 3 = (0.58, 0, 0, 0.58, 0, 0, 0.58). Rows 1 and 2 → 1.00: the same direction (0.71 × 0.71 + 0.71 × 0.71 = 1.00, with the unrounded 0.707). Rows 1 and 3 → 0.00: no position where both are non-zero, so every product is 0; they are at right angles.
