Natural Language Processing

CSE 4811 / DS 4213, Fall 2026

Muhammad Anwarul Azim, Lecturer, Department of Computer Science and Engineering
Room: 619 (D)

Natural Language Processing is the study of how machines read, write and understand human language. In this course students build language models step by step: n-gram models, word vectors, recurrent networks, and the transformer. Every model is trained on Bangla and English text, and the results are compared on a class leaderboard that runs through the term. Later classes open the trained model and ask what it has learned, and cover tokenization, scaling, interpretability, evaluation and post-training. By the end of the course, students will be able to read a current research paper, reproduce its main result and judge whether the claim holds. They will also understand how chat assistants such as ChatGPT and Claude are built, where the field stands today, and where it is moving.
